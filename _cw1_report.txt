Abstract 
 
Accurate classification of goods using the Harmonized System (HS) is essential for international 
trade, yet Sri Lanka Customs currently relies on inefficient, manual processes involving static 
PDF documents and printed tariff books. This lack of digitalization results in time consuming 
searches, duty calculation errors and operational bottlenecks for importers, exporters and 
customs officers. This project presents “CeylonHS” an intelligent web based HS code finder 
designed to modernize and streamline the classification process. 
 
The proposed solution utilizes a semantic search engine powered by Machine Learning (ML), 
specifically integrating the Typesense engine with all-MiniLM-l12-v2 embedding model to 
enable context aware keyword matching rather than simple text lookups. The system architecture 
employs a Flask backend with a React-Based frontend, ensuring a responsive user interface that 
displays HS codes, duty rates and matching confidence scores. Developed using Agile 
methodology to facilitate iterative improvement and Object Oriented Analysis and Design 
(OOAD) for architectural modularity, CeylonHS aims to bridge the technology gap in Sri 
Lanka’s trade infrastructure. By providing a centralized, transparent and data driven platform. 
This system seeks to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of customs operations,aligning with 
national digital transformation goals 
 
Keywords: HS code. Machine learning, Semantic Search, Sri Lanka Customs, Agile 
Methodology, Trade Facilitation

Chapter 1: Introduction 
1.2 Problem Background 
The Harmonized System (HS) of tariff classification is a globalma, and importing 
datasets.system developed by the World Customs Organization (WCO). Every country uses HS 
codes to classify goods for customs clearance, taxation and trade statistics. For Sri Lanka, 
accurate HS classification is essential not only for calculating import duties and taxes but also for 
maintaining transparency, ensuring trade compliance and preventing revenue losses. 
 
However Sri Lanka Customs’ current HS classification process remains outdated and inefficient. 
At present most importer,exporter and brokers rely on large PDF documents or printed tariff 
books published by Sri Lanka Customs, which list thousands of codes with minimal search 
capability. Users must manually scan these documents to identify the correct classification, a 
process that is time-consuming, error-prone and discouraging for small and medium size traders. 
 
This lack of an intelligent digital system has created a gap in trade facilitation and operational 
efficiency. Inconsistent classification often leads to incorrect duty calculations, delays in 
clearance, and frequent disputes between traders and custom officers. Furthermore, without a 
centralized searchable platform there is limited transparency for the public and unnecessary 
workload for customs officers who must handle repetitive classification inquiries daily. 
 
Globally many customs administrations have already adopted online HS code search engines and 
Ai driven classification systems that simplify this process. Systems such as Singapore’s 
TradeNet, Indian customs, Dubai’s Al Munasiq and Zonos AI HS code offer users intelligent 
keyword search, category filter, and automated suggestions based on machine learning. These 
systems not only  improve accessibility for traders but also enhance compliance and accuracy 
operations. 
 
In contrast, Sri Lanka lacks a dedicated online HS code lookup system with intelligent 
capabilities. While there are ongoing digitalization efforts through the National Single Window 
(NSW) and ASYCUDA World, these platforms mainly focus on submission and processing of 
declarations rather than classifications rather than classification assistance. Therefore a 
specialized, AI-enhanced HS Code HS Code Finder tailored to the Sri Lankan context is needed 
to bridge this digital gap and support the modernization goals of the Department of Customs. 
 
1.3 Problem Statement 
 
The absence of a centralized, intelligent HS code search platform in Sri Lanka Customs results in 
inefficient product classification, increased clearance delays, and reduced transparency in import 
and export processes.

1.4 Proposed Solution 
The proposed solution is a smart, web-based HS Code Finder System designed to modernize and 
simplify the process of identifying Harmonized System (HS) codes within Sri Lanka Customs. 
The system will serve as an intuitive search engine where users can type a product name or brief 
description. For example, “laptop computer”, “Apple Iphone”, or “green tea” and instantly view 
the corresponding HS code, official tariff description, and applicable duty rate.  
 
This solution aims to provide an intelligent, fast, and transparent classification experience that 
supports both general users (importers, exporters, and logistics partners) and customs officers. 
Instead of relying on static PDF tariff documents or manual searches, users will be able to access 
all classification data through a centralized and searchable web platform that delivers accurate 
and validated results.  
 
Key proposed features include:  
 
●​ Smart keyword search: Uses advanced text-processing or AI-based matching 
algorithms to understand product descriptions and suggest the most relevant HS codes.  
 
●​ Matching-accuracy indicator: Displays a confidence or similarity score for each 
suggested code, helping users judge the relevance of each result.  
 
●​ Filter and refine options: Allows users to narrow results by product category, trade 
sector, or duty percentage.  
 
●​ Secure officer login (planned feature): Enables authorized customs officers to log in, 
verify, or update HS entries when necessary.  
 
●​ AI chatbot (planned enhancement): Provides conversational assistance, helping users 
refine their search queries and understand tariff-related information.  
 
●​ Responsive and accessible interface: Optimized for both desktop and mobile users, 
ensuring usability across diverse devices and connection speeds.  
The system will be built with a scalable architecture so that it can later integrate with other 
national digital platforms such as the National Single Window or ASYCUDA World, aligning 
with Sri Lanka’s ongoing efforts toward digital transformation in trade and customs 
administration. ​
In essence, this project introduces a new, data-driven digital tool that enhances transparency, 
reduces manual workload, and supports faster, more accurate classification decisions across the 
Sri Lankan customs ecosystem.

CeylonHS (Proposed Solution) 
 
CeylonHS positions itself as a next-generation compliance assistant designed to simplify the 
complexity of HS code determination. Built with a strong AI and Machine Learning engine, the 
tool allows users to input product details via natural descriptions or image recognition, instantly 
generating the most accurate HS code. Unlike static lookup tools, CeylonHS  focuses on 
context-aware classification, offering a confidence score with each result to help users gauge 
accuracy and mitigate risk. Designed for flexibility, it supports both single lookups for quick 
checks and bulk processing capabilities, making it an ideal solution for businesses looking to 
streamline their customs operations and ensure seamless regulatory compliance across multiple 
jurisdictions.

Dubai Customs: AI Munasiq 
Dubai Customs offers a highly advanced AI-powered tool called AI Munasiq. This proprietary 
application leverages machine learning to help users find the correct HS code by simply typing a 
description or, more impressively, by uploading an image of the product. The AI analyzes the 
visual or text input and predicts the appropriate HS code along with applicable customs duties 
and any prohibition or restriction status. It is designed to learn from user interactions to improve 
its accuracy over time and is fully integrated into Dubai’s smart government ecosystem to 
facilitate rapid trade clearance. 
 
 
Singapore: HS/CA Product Code Search 
Singapore customs utilizes the HS/CA Product Code Checker, a tool hosted on the GoBusiness 
platform that has been enhanced with Large-Language Modeling(LLM) capabilities. This 
AI-driven search allows traders to input product description in natural language to receive 
recommended 8-digit HS codes. Uniquely, this tool bridges the gap between customs and other 
regulatory bodies by simultaneously identifying the Competent Authority (CA) Product Code. 
This ensures that traders are immediately informed if their goods require specific licenses or 
approvals from agencies like the Singapore Food Agency or Health Sciences Authority, making 
it a comprehensive compliance tool. 
 
India: ICEGATE (Trade Guide on imports) 
India’s ICEGATE portal offers the “Trade Guide on Imports,” which is built to handle the 
complex  multi- layered tax  structure of Indian Custom. The system's main requirement and 
feature is its ability to calculate the total “Effective Duty,” which aggregates the  Basic Customs 
Duty  (BCD),Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST), and other surcharges  into a final 
payable amount.  This financial accuracy makes it a powerful tool for calculating the true 
“landed cost” of imports. However , the product suffers from significant usability weaknesses; 
the user interface is considered   complex, and the search engine features  a “Strict Search” 
mechanism that often struggles with non technical keywords for example, failing to find results 
for “Phone” unless the term “Telephonic apparatus” is  Used.  
 
 
 
 
Zonos (Commercial / Third-Party Model)  
Zonos operates as a commercial technology platform rather than a government repository, 
addressing the requirements of automated,high-volume classification for e-commerce merchants. 
Its main feature is an  AI driven “Classify” engine that utilizes the Natural Language Processing

3.1 Chapter overview 
 
This chapter explains the methodologies and frameworks adopted for the successful development 
of the HS Code Finder System for Sri Lanka Customs. It covers the project’s development 
model, design and management methodologies, and the tools used to organize teamwork and 
mitigate potential risks.  
 
The chosen methodologies aim to ensure that the project is structured, efficient, and scalable, 
while maintaining clear communication and predictable progress throughout the system 
lifecycle. Each phase from data preparation to system deployment follows a clearly defined 
approach to ensure quality assurance and traceability at every stage.  
 
 
3.2 Development Methodology  
A Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) model provides a structured plan for creating 
software, acting as a step by step roadmap for the entire project. It covers every stage of the 
process, starting from analyzing what the users need, to designing the system, writing the code, 
testing for errors, and finally  delivering the applications. Selecting the best model is a key 
decision for the project’s success. It determines the daily workflow, how the project team 
communicates and how the software handles changes in requirements.  
 
For the Sri Lanka Customs HS Code Finder system, the team selected the Agile Software 
Development Methodology. This approach divided the project into short, focused work cycles 
known as ‘sprints’. Agile was chosen because  the system’s core feature, the ‘Smart Search 
Engine' algorithm, requires continuous testing and refinement to ensure accuracy. Unlike rigid 
models, Agile allows the team to release a basic version first and progressively improve the 
search logic and User interface based on feedback. This flexibility ensures that changes to 
requirements or data can be managed  efficiently without affecting the project timeline.

3.3 Design Methodology 
The development of any system adheres to a systematic framework that encompasses established 
principles and procedures, collectively known as a design methodology. These methodologies 
facilitate structured approaches to software specification by reducing implementation 
complexities, enhancing project focus, and clarifying requirement definitions (Sommerville, 
2016). The primary aim is to deliver solutions that are both practical and efficient while 
thoroughly addressing end-user requirements. Such methodological frameworks are applicable 
across various domains, including both software applications and physical products. In the 
context of developing the HS Code Search Engine, two predominant methodologies merit 
examination for their relevance to modeling, analysis, and problem resolution: SSADM and 
OOAD.

3.3.1 SSADM (Structured Systems Analysis and Design Method) 
The Structured Systems Analysis and Design Method (SSADM) serves as a systematic 
framework for managing and directing the development processes of information systems. 
Originating in the United Kingdom in the early 1980s as a government initiative, this 
methodology subsequently became the mandated standard for information technology 
acquisitions within the public sector (Avison & Fitzgerald, 2006). SSADM operates through 
clearly defined developmental stages: initial requirements elicitation, comprehensive analysis, 
system design, and final implementation. This phased approach guarantees the delivery of robust 
and reliable systems. 
 
 
Figure 5: A Generic Data Flow Diagram (DFD) typical of the SSADM process. Source: 
EdrawSoft 
Within the scope of this project, SSADM is especially pertinent for establishing the sequential 
data processing pipeline essential for managing Customs information. The operational workflow 
encompasses several distinct stages: the initial extraction of data from published HS Code PDF 
documents, transformation into a structured persistent dataset, data cleansing operations, 
semantic enrichment through external API integration for synonym identification, and the final

preparation through vectorization for search index integration. This progressive data 
transformation sequence, which transitions from unprocessed source materials to the final 
Typesense search infrastructure, aligns closely with SSADM’s characteristic sequential 
development philosophy (Kendall & Kendall, 2014). 
 
3.3.2 OOAD (Object-Oriented Analysis and Design) 
Object-Oriented Analysis and Design (OOAD) represents a paradigm that conceptualizes 
software systems through discrete entities that reflect real-world objects and their interactions. 
This methodology begins with a comprehensive requirement analysis, proceeds to identify 
fundamental system objects, and establishes the interaction patterns among these components. 
The resulting architecture enhances system maintainability, promotes component reusability, and 
facilitates future expansion (Booch et al., 2007).

supports sustainable system evolution and operational efficiency over time (Larman, 2004).

3.3.3 Methodology Selection: SSADM or OOAD? 
 
The HS Code Search Engine necessitates architectural flexibility, component modularity, and 
evolutionary capability as data processing demands evolve over time. A comprehensive 
evaluation of both methodologies against project requirements indicates that Object-Oriented 
Analysis and Design demonstrates superior suitability due to its inherent adaptability. 
While the data processing pipeline exhibits characteristics conducive to SSADM’s structured 
approach, the broader system functionality requires a more flexible architectural foundation. 
Specifically, the needs for autonomous local deployment, automated data synchronization, and 
interactive 
administrative 
features—including 
dataset 
editing 
and 
version 
rollback 
capabilities—demand the architectural modularity that OOAD provides (Pressman & Maxim, 
2015). The foundational principles of encapsulation, component reusability, and extensibility 
inherent in OOAD render it optimally suited for accommodating the system’s continuous 
evolution and long-term maintenance needs. 
 
3.4 Project Management Methodology 
 
3.4.1 What is a Project Management Methodology 
A project management methodology is a structured framework that includes specific processes, 
techniques, and tools designed to guide a project from inception to completion. It serves as a 
strategic roadmap for the project team, clearly defining how work is prioritized, how 
communication flows, and how risks are managed to ensure consistency and control throughout 
the lifecycle, Whether using a traditional linear model or a mixed approach such as a hybrid, the 
primary objective of any methodology is to standardize execution, reduce uncertainly, and ensure 
that the final product is delivered within the agreed constraints of time, cost and quality. 
 
3.4.2 Agile Methodology 
 
For this project, the Agile Methodology was chosen as the primary management framework due 
to the high adaptability and need for continuous iteration throughout the development lifecycle. 
In contrast to strictly linear or rigid approaches, Agile replaces the sequential structure of 
traditional models with iterative cycles for planning, design, development,  and testing phases 
known as sprints. 
 
This approach is particularly beneficial to our team as it facilitates incremental delivery of the 
CeylonHS search engine, allowing immediate testing and rapid optimization of the user interface

collaboration, this flexibility effectively reduces the risk of delivering a product that fails to align

requirements. Furthermore, the methodology’s emphasis on regular retrospective reviews and 
continuous integration ensures that issues are identified and addressed promptly, guaranteeing 
that the final system is robust and deeply aligned with the practical needs of Sri Lanka Customs 
users.
